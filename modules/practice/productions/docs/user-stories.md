# productions: user stories

Written in the generic words of the contract. Each academy's profile vocabulary renames them: a
*production* is a concert at a music academy, an exhibition at an arts academy, a hackathon at an
engineering college, a moot at a law school.

## Putting something on

1. **As a production manager**, I add a production as soon as it is agreed, before the hall or the
   dates are settled, so that everybody can see it is coming.
2. **As a production manager**, I give a production its dates, venue and call time when they are
   settled, and I can move them afterwards without losing the cast.
3. **As a stage manager**, I am warned when I schedule something with less than a week to prepare,
   but I am not stopped: sometimes that is the decision.
4. **As a production manager**, I cancel a production with a reason, and everybody holding a part
   stops holding it.
5. **As a production manager**, I close a production once it has been performed, and nothing can
   change it afterwards.
6. **As a production manager**, if my browser retries a creation I get the production I already
   made, not a duplicate — and never somebody else's.

## Being in it

7. **As a director**, I offer a part to a performer and they decide; I do not mark them as cast
   myself.
8. **As a performer**, I see on my dashboard which parts I have been offered and which I still have
   to answer, with the dates and the call time.
9. **As a performer**, I accept or decline a part, and I may give a reason for declining.
10. **As a performer**, I cannot answer a part offered to somebody else, and I cannot change my
    answer once given.
11. **As a director**, I withdraw an assignment when I recast, with a reason, and I can offer that
    part again later to the same person.
12. **As a director**, if two of us act on the same part at the same moment, only one of us wins and
    the other is told, rather than both changes applying.

## Getting it ready

13. **As a choreographer**, I keep the list of what must be ready before the production — costumes,
    lights, programmes — with a due date and who is doing each.
14. **As a mentor**, my dashboard puts the production in the most trouble first: the one with
    overdue tasks, then the one happening soonest.
15. **As a lead**, I see at a glance what is outstanding on each production: how many tasks are
    overdue, how many are open, how many invitations nobody has answered.
16. **As a lead**, a production with nothing outstanding tells me it is ready to schedule.

## Seeing what is on

17. **As a student**, my dashboard shows the next productions of my academy so that I know what is
    coming, without showing me who is in them.
18. **As a coordinating office**, my calendar shows what is on over the next weeks with the venue,
    the time and how many of the cast have confirmed.
19. **As anyone**, a production with no dates yet is shown as such, not as a blank or a broken date.
20. **As anyone**, when the service is unavailable my dashboard says so and offers to try again,
    rather than showing an empty widget as though nothing were on.

## Keeping tenants apart

21. **As an organisation**, no other organisation's production, part or task is ever visible to my
    people, by listing or by guessing an id — and an id from another organisation answers 404, which
    does not confirm that it exists.
